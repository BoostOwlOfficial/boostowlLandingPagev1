When we started building BoostOwl, we didn't plan to write a blog. We thought we'd just build the product, ship it, and let the work speak.

It turned out we had more to say than that.

## Why we're writing

We've spent the last year building software for a kind of business most SaaS founders never see up close — the cricket grounds, helmet shops, neighbourhood clinics and tuition centres that hold up the Indian economy quietly. These aren't tech-forward businesses. They're WhatsApp-forward businesses. The CRM is the owner's phone. The booking system is a paper diary. The marketing channel is a Status update.

Building for them is humbling. You quickly learn that nothing you read on Twitter applies. Nobody is debating whether to use Server Components or Resend. They're debating whether the chai shop next door is cheating them out of customers.

We want to write about that.

## What you can expect here

A few kinds of posts:

- **Case studies.** What a real BoostOwl customer's week looks like, the messy details included.
- **Product notes.** Why we built a feature, what we got wrong, what we changed.
- **Guides.** Plain-English explainers — the WhatsApp Business API, GST invoicing, dynamic pricing, the things our customers ask us about over chai.
- **Founder notes.** What we're thinking about, what we're worried about, what we got excited about this week.

We'll try to write the way we talk to customers — direct, specific, allergic to jargon.

## A note on tone

We're not going to pretend we have it all figured out. We've been live for a few months with one paying customer and two more onboarding. Most of what we're going to write will be us thinking out loud about what we're learning. Some of it will age badly. That's fine.

If you're an SMB owner, an operator, or just curious — we hope this is useful.
